The prototype

From idea to experience.

The Mind Matters prototype is an approximately two-hour workshop combining presentation-based learning, worksheets, games and practical activities.

Feedback Improvements

What's been built so far.

The workshop exists as a complete, deliverable session — a presentation, a set of activities and games, worksheets, and facilitated discussion built around realistic conflict scenarios.

Everything below is drawn from the actual prototype. Where materials haven't been published on this site yet, we've left a clearly marked placeholder rather than showing a stand-in image.

Feedback

What people told us.

We sent the finished prototype — presentation, worksheets and activities — to Year 3–6 teachers and a Year 3 student, and asked them to be honest about it.

From teachers

What teachers responded to

  • Content pitched correctly for the age and developmental stage
  • Covers the conflicts students genuinely encounter at this age
  • Strategies that work during a real conflict, not just in discussion
  • Worksheets most students can complete independently
  • Emotions, regulation and conflict resolution covered in one session

Rated 4 out of 5 stars.

What we were asked to change

  • More scenarios, particularly friendship situations for Years 5–6
  • Model a scenario first, then let groups work through their own and report back
  • Demonstrate the strategies rather than only describing them
  • Take-home and classroom resources — calming cards, posters, handouts
  • Connect to strategies students already use, such as High Five
  • A follow-up lesson to revisit and practise

Critical feedback — from a Year 3 student

The student found the activities useful, but said the workshop itself was boring, that they already knew most of the content, and rated it 3 out of 5.

That's the most useful feedback we received. Teachers assessed whether the content was right; the student told us whether it actually held their attention — and it didn't.

Improvements

What we're changing, and why.

Feedback is only useful if it changes something. Each change below traces directly back to something a teacher or student told us.

1
Cut the reading, add the doing

Less to read, more to do.

Our Year 3 reviewer called the workshop boring and asked for “more activities and less information to read.” We're cutting text-heavy slides and breaking the remaining content into shorter chunks, so students are never reading for long.

2
Model it first

Demonstrate a scenario before students try one.

Both teachers asked for this. We'll work through one scenario in front of the group, then hand out different scenarios for small groups to solve and report back on — rather than explaining a strategy and hoping it lands.

3
More scenarios

Friendship scenarios pitched for Years 5–6.

A teacher noted that older students face more complex friendship issues — loyalty, exclusion, shifting groups — than our current scenarios cover. We're adding scenarios aimed specifically at Years 5–6.

4
Something to keep

Take-home and classroom resources.

Both teachers asked for calming cards, classroom posters and a handout of the key strategies that students can take home or glue into a book — so the language stays in the room after we leave.

5
Build on what they know

Connect to strategies the school already uses.

A teacher suggested linking to approaches students already recognise, such as the High Five strategy. Making those connections explicit helps students see these as an extension of what they know, not a separate set of rules.

6
Make it stick

An optional follow-up session.

One teacher described the workshop as “an excellent starting point” and suggested a follow-up lesson to revisit the strategies and practise applying them in new situations. We're scoping this as a second session schools can add.

Where these stand

These are changes we've committed to from the feedback, not changes already built into the prototype shown above. We'd rather be clear about the difference.
